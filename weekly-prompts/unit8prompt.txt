1. Chapter 15 Topics

"Colormaps are used extensively with scientific data.
How are these applied when visualizing measurable phenomenon like temperatures or rainfall? 
Reference your textbook or the internet. Cite examples that you find interesting."

According to the textbook, a colormap is a sequence of colors in a gradient that moves from a starting color to an ending one. 
They are used in visualizations to display and highlight patterns in data through the use of color. 
For example, you could show low or high points in data by making them brighter or dimmer colors in the colormap.
Colormaps are applied to various phenomena across a wide variety of data, many of which are encountered in daily life.
A weather radar map used to showcase a storm is a type of colormap that showcases radar reflectivity using yellows, greens, and reds.
Another example of colormaps can be found in thermal imaging cameras, which often use a specific color palette named "Ironbow".
The ironbow color palette uses lighter colors to highlight hotter temperatures, and darker and cooler colors for colder temperatures.
Medical imaging is another area where colormaps are used. Doppler ultrasound uses a specific colormap to show blood flow.
Flow that moves towards the probe is visualized with shades of red, orange, and yellow, while flow moving away uses shades of blue.
The colormap also uses the brightness of the colors to show speed, with brighter colors meaning faster flow, and darker colors meaning slower flow.
This allows people to quickly identify any potential blood blockages, since they will be differentiated in appearance.
